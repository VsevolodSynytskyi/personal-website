## Kyiv

I was born and raised in Kyiv.

In my first year (2020), I dropped out of  NaUKMA — Faculty of Informatics, Software Engineering — to focus on work.

Back then, I worked as a graphic designer.

## Odesa

In 2021, I had an interview:
* Do you live in Odesa?
* No, why do you ask?
* Well, we’re opening an office in Odesa.

So, at 19, I moved to Odesa — a German agency was opening a new office there.

I moved out of my rented apartment in Kyiv a month before the move, so by June 2021, I was technically homeless.
During that time, I decided to hop between hostels across Ukraine — eight cities in a month.

While working in Odesa for the Germans, I also freelanced. That’s how I accidentally met Andrew — who later became my boss.

I did a few gigs for him and asked to join full-time.

German pragmatism was replaced by a garage-startup American vibe — which suits me better.

Since then, I’ve been working on software for construction project management.

## War

2022 — war. Like for everyone.

I spent the first months of the invasion in Lviv.

There, my friend and I built a volunteer service — we helped drive IDPs during curfew.
I wrote more about it here (link).
We helped about 1,500 people reach shelter.
Funny how basically only we and our volunteer drivers know about it.

Later that year, I settled in Ivano-Frankivsk.

During the 2022–2023 winter blackouts, we opened a coworking space — with a generator, Starlink, blackjack, and hookers.

Except no hookers. And no blackjack.

That’s also when, in Ivano-Frankivsk, I found Nora — my dog.

## Carpathians

In the summer of 2023, we closed the coworking, and I moved with Nora to the Carpathians — renting a hut twenty kilometers from Kosiv (home of the Kosiv market and Kosiv ceramics).

I wanted some solitude to think about what to do next.

There, I enrolled in a driving school in Kosiv.

A wise person once told me:

> Vsevolod, if you want to drive a car, you need a car, not a license.

So I bought a little jeep in the village and learned to drive in it.
Now this phrase gives me determination beyond driving.

It’s nothing like circling a mall parking lot. When you tame a Carpathian beater — if you didn’t die, you learned.
